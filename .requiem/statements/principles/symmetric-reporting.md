Results are reported in the same plain terms whichever way they point, for or against believers' or critics' claims. An inconclusive result is called inconclusive, never rounded into support or refutation. Every tested hypothesis is run against its strongest competitor through the same measurement (e.g. Egyptian comparisons alongside Joseph Smith's own English and period pastiches). Report prose describes what was measured and what it rules out, without editorialising. Constitution I.
